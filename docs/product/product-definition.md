# Product definition

## Vision

Beaver AI is an AI-powered personalized learning platform that helps each
student understand their current knowledge, close prerequisite gaps, choose an
effective next step, practice toward durable mastery, and make informed
academic decisions.

Its defining promise is not simply access to content or a chatbot. Beaver AI
builds a coherent learning experience from structured educational content,
evidence of student learning, individual goals, and appropriately constrained
AI assistance.

## Product principles

1. **Student-first.** Optimize for understanding, agency, accessibility, and
   healthy learning—not engagement at any cost.
2. **Mastery over completion.** Completing an activity is evidence of
   participation, not proof of mastery.
3. **Personalization.** Adapt sequence, support, practice, and review to the
   learner while preserving clear educational goals.
4. **AI as an educational tool.** Use AI to teach, guide, and reveal reasoning,
   not to bypass productive effort or provide ungrounded answers.
5. **Structured educational context.** Connect AI interactions to approved
   content, learning objectives, prerequisites, and relevant learner context.
6. **Explainability.** Give understandable reasons for important
   recommendations and educational decisions where practical.
7. **Safety and privacy.** Minimize risk and data collection because students,
   including minors, are a core audience.
8. **Extensibility.** Establish stable boundaries that later capabilities can
   extend without forcing a complete rewrite.
9. **Simplicity.** Choose the least complex design that meets known needs and
   supports the next credible phase.
10. **Coherence.** Keep product behavior, language, design, code, tests, data,
    and documentation synchronized.

## Target users

### Primary users

- **Students:** initially school-age and test-preparation learners seeking
  guided study, explanations, practice, and visible progress. Age range and
  first launch segment remain a product decision before onboarding is built.

### Supporting users

- **Parents or guardians:** future consent, safety, and progress visibility
  appropriate to the learner's age and jurisdiction.
- **Educators and content specialists:** future content authoring, review,
  assignment, interpretation, and intervention workflows.
- **Administrators and safety operators:** future policy, support, moderation,
  audit, and operational controls.

Supporting roles are not all promised launch roles. Permissions and
relationships must be defined before implementation in the relevant phase.

## User outcomes

Beaver AI should help a student:

- form an accurate picture of strengths, gaps, and progress;
- understand why a next activity is recommended;
- receive explanations suited to current knowledge and goals;
- practice at an appropriate challenge level;
- revisit knowledge before it is forgotten;
- remain motivated without manipulation or shame; and
- build increasing confidence and independent learning skill.

## Core experience loop

The long-term experience follows a transparent evidence loop:

1. **Orient:** understand the student's goals, context, and current plan.
2. **Diagnose:** gather evidence without assuming a single score tells the
   whole story.
3. **Recommend:** select and explain a useful next learning action.
4. **Learn:** engage with reviewed content and context-aware guidance.
5. **Practice:** apply knowledge with feedback and appropriate scaffolding.
6. **Assess:** collect stronger evidence of understanding and transfer.
7. **Update:** revise mastery estimates, misconceptions, and the learning plan.
8. **Reflect and review:** show progress and schedule retention work.

The loop is a product model, not a requirement that every session use every
step. Students must be able to understand and influence their path.

## Experience qualities

- Friendly, calm, capable, and encouraging
- Focused on one useful next step rather than an overwhelming dashboard
- Honest about uncertainty and the source of recommendations
- Accessible across ability, language, input method, and device constraints
- Engaging through meaningful progress rather than compulsive mechanics
- Nature-inspired—forest, river, wood, sage, and beaver themes—without becoming
  childish or visually noisy

## Success measures

Future product metrics should balance:

- demonstrated learning and retention;
- calibration of mastery estimates;
- student ability to explain and transfer knowledge;
- completion of self-chosen learning goals;
- safety, trust, accessibility, and learner-reported helpfulness; and
- equitable outcomes across supported student groups.

Time-on-platform, streak length, or answer volume are not sufficient success
measures and must never displace learning quality or well-being.

## Assumptions and unresolved product choices

Phase 0 assumes a web-first experience and eventual support for minors, so the
strict safety posture applies from the start. Before Phase 1 or Phase 2 reaches
production readiness, product owners must choose the initial learner age band,
launch jurisdiction, guardian/educator role scope, accessibility target, and
initial subject or curriculum. Those choices affect consent, content, data
retention, and onboarding and should be captured in decision records.
